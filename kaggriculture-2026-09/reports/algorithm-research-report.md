# Algorithm choice for the Kaggle farm/market simulation

**Method:** `web_search` was broken all session; everything came from direct `web_fetch`/`curl` against arXiv, Nature, GitHub and Kaggle. Links resolved; unverified items flagged.

## 0. What actually won comparable competitions

- **Lux AI S3 rank 1 was "Flat Neurons"**, using **IMPALA multi-agent RL with large-scale self-play** — frozen opponent pool, teacher KL, ResNet/ConvLSTM/Transformer, **~1.5B env steps final, "over 20B across all experiments", 3–4 days of GPU training** ([write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/lux-ai-season-3/writeups/flat-neurons-1st-place-approach-by-flat-neurons), [code](https://github.com/tonykozlovsky/lux-ai3-pub)). **You cannot afford this**, and it needed a compact action space (16 units × 6 actions + a sap-target head) that you do not have.
- **Ranks 3, 4 and 8 were imitation learning**: rank 3 distilled two U-Nets from **Frog Parade + Flat Neurons** replays, and reports IL beat his best rule-based bot after "a couple of hours of training" ([#3](https://github.com/andreyd41/lux3-bot), [#4](https://github.com/KASSII/Kaggle_LuxAI-s3), [#8](https://github.com/gregorlied/lux-s3)).
- **Across the category the pattern is the opposite of the brief's expectation: hand-tuned heuristics + heavy offline search.** **Lux AI S2 rank 1 is 43 hand-written files, zero neural nets**: 11 role state machines plus **forward simulation of the real engine ~2.9 s per call → 5–50+ steps of lookahead** ([write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/c/lux-ai-season-2/discussion/407982), [code](https://github.com/ryandy/Lux-S2-public)). Halite I–IV were heuristics + simulated annealing / hill-climbing over a forward sim / Dijkstra ([Halite II](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/halite-ii-concludes-winners-announced/)); **Kore 2022 rank 1 "focus[ed] on the rule-based approach"**, margin 15.8 Elo over 2nd ([write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kore-2022/discussion/340035)).
- **No winner used MCTS/AlphaZero, MAP-Elites, CMA-ES, MILP/CP-SAT, or LLM-driven search.** The winning "search" is **bounded lookahead inside a hand-written planner** — exactly your situation.
- **FunSearch on Google Hash Code** is the closest match to your *resource* profile: evolving a scoring function for a human-written greedy backbone reached **top percentile in all 8 rounds and beat the top human team in 5**, within **~10,500 evaluations in 2 hours** ([Veličković et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19744)).
- **Policy quality beats search quantity**: policy-guided MCTS beat baseline MCTS in **97% of Hex games** at a mere 2× slowdown, while **doubling raw MCTS iterations bought only 56%** ([ExIt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439)). MCTS scaling also saturates — self-play success *falls* 71.1% → 58.5% from 1,000 → 256,000 simulations ([Bourki et al. 2010](https://icl.utk.edu/users/herault/papers/067%20-%20Scalability%20and%20Parallelization%20of%20Monte-Carlo%20Tree%20Search%20-%20CG%20(2010).pdf)). **Root-parallel** gives 14.9× strength-speedup on 16 cores vs 2.6–8.5× tree-parallel ([Chaslot et al.](https://dke.maastrichtuniversity.nl/m.winands/documents/multithreadedMCTS2.pdf)).

## 1. Ranked families

1. **Imitation learning from replays + distillation.** [DAgger](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html) · [AlphaStar](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z). Clones strong teams' policies from ladder replays, then improves them on your simulator. **Fits because** Lux S3 ranks 3/4/8 did exactly this while rank 1 needed a GPU farm; CPU-only; you already have replays. **Cost: low-moderate** — observation reconstruction, not the network, dominates. *Flag: pure BC regret is quadratic in horizon (T²ε), so at 720 steps it plateaus near the teacher; DAgger is the fix.*

2. **Receding-horizon CEM planning over your *known* simulator, then distil it.** [PETS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12114). CEM planning on a known model matches model-free asymptotics with **8–125× fewer samples**, and beat random shooting at equal sample count; MPPI samples "on a GPU" and only wins at very large exploration variance — skip it. **Fits because** you have a known model and 104 cores: run CEM as the *planner/teacher*, DAgger the result into a fast policy. **Do NOT build MCTS/ExIt:** ExIt needed ~10,000 rollouts per move ≈ **7.2M simulations per game** at 720 steps. **Cost: moderate.** *This best explains the rank-1 fingerprint.*

3. **CMA-ES / ES over a small macro-template parameter vector.** [Hansen & Ostermeier](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00772) · [pycma](https://github.com/CMA-ES/pycma). ES is "tolerant of extremely long horizons" — but only in low dimension. OpenAI's ES was ~10× *worse* in sample efficiency, winning only via 1,440 cores vs 32, and fails on sparse-reward barriers. **Cost: very low.** *You have likely saturated this.*

4. **Market-specific opponent modelling and exploitation.** [Calvano et al., *AER* 110(10)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20190623) found Q-learners reliably reach supracompetitive prices, but collusion is **not robust under sufficient exploration** ([NBER w28535](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28535)), and the only 2-firm study finds **asynchrony hampers** it. Separately, single-agent execution models are **structurally misspecified** once the counterparty adapts — the problem "ceases to be a single-agent control problem and instead becomes a finite-horizon stochastic game" ([arXiv:2511.02016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02016)). **Cost: low-moderate.** *Test adversarially; expect it to vanish.*

5. **LLM-driven evolutionary program search** — [FunSearch, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6) · [AlphaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131) · [EoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02051) (2,000 queries, single CPU) · [ReEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01145) · [ShinkaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19349) · [OpenEvolve](https://github.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve) (~$0.01–0.60/iteration, CPU-only). **Fits because** it needs exactly what you have: a cheap *scalar* evaluator and pure-Python programs. *Flags: 16.6% measured evaluator-hacking rate; plateaus by ~20–50 generations; LLM latency, not eval cost, binds.*

6. **CP-SAT / MILP rolling-horizon scheduling** over a bounded crop/labour subproblem. [OR-Tools](https://github.com/google/or-tools). **Fit is adversarial**: exact only for deterministic, fully-observed integer subproblems, and your opponent invalidates any plan. `num_search_workers` defaults to a **randomised portfolio** → non-reproducible unless you set `interleave_search`. Use only for a static single-player crop-plan bound.

7. **MAP-Elites / quality-diversity.** [Mouret & Clune](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909) · [Cully et al., *Nature* 521:503](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14422) · [pyribs](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs). **Fits poorly as a learner**: the founding archive needed **40M evaluations ≈ 2 weeks on one multi-core machine** (~1,900 h at your throughput), plain MAP-Elites is "limited to low-dimensional controllers", and its scaling fixes are ES- or GPU-based. **One pattern transfers**: that work's *exploitation* phase needed only **~17 trials** to reach 90% of max performance — freeze a small library of macro-schedule variants offline and select among them at inference. **No measured QD win rate exists in any 2-player game.**

8. **Deep RL (PPO / AlphaZero / league self-play).** [AlphaStar league](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z) · [mini-AlphaStar](https://github.com/liuruoze/mini-AlphaStar). AlphaZero needed 5,000 TPUs; the cheapest credible reimplementation still needed ~3 days on a K80 for 6×6 Othello ([alpha-zero-general](https://github.com/suragnair/alpha-zero-general)). League *exploiters* only pay off in cyclic strategy spaces and are themselves the source of non-transitivity. **Do not restart this** — it failed for the structural reason (huge per-step branching × terminal-only reward). *Cheap exception: PFSP weighting over your own checkpoints, in the pipeline.*

9. **CFR / mean-field games.** [Deep CFR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00164); regret scales with |infosets| × √|A|, and Deep CFR self-warns external sampling is impractical at very large branching factors. Mean-field games are N→∞ and **invalid at N=2** — at N=2 the mean field *is* the opponent. **Skip.**

## 2. What rank-1 most likely is (weighted)

1. **~60% — hand-written closed-loop scheduler + fixed macro template, with final-turn planning over the *known* engine and a small tuned parameter set.** Best fit: identical hire ladder and land days (template), 3× batch-size variation (closed loop on shop state), 99% utilisation (greedy assignment), 20 s on the **last turn only** (bounded receding-horizon planning, affordable exactly once).
2. **~25% — the same scheduler plus a small offline-learned value model** ranking final-turn options.
3. **~15% — a replay-cloned policy** wrapped in the template above.

**Single discriminating experiment:** re-simulate a rank-1 replay with the opponent replaced by a scripted baseline and measure whether its *action sequence* changes. Opponent-invariant actions but a last-turn action tracking its own final inventory → template + final-turn planning (1/2). Actions tracking opponent state mid-game → opponent-conditioned policy (3).

**Run this first — cheap and high-signal: an exploitability test.** [Lisý & Bowling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07547) found top poker bots exploitable by extreme margins: "with 97.5% confidence, exploitability of each evaluated bot is over 3180 mBB/h", versus 750 mBB/h for folding every hand — **folding beats their strategy by ≥4×** against worst case. Cheap method: maintain a Bayesian range over opponent state, renormalise after each observed action, take a greedy one-step best response. Your PPO failure is consistent with an exploitable policy, so this diagnoses rank-1 *and* tests whether a best-response agent is the win. Caveat: lower bound — pair with head-to-head play.

## 3. One-week pipeline

- **Days 1–2 (no GPU).** Reconstruct the replay observation space; train a BC policy on top-team replays. *Measure:* action-agreement with teacher, win rate vs your scripted agent.
- **Days 2–4 (21k games/h).** Add receding-horizon CEM over the known engine, then DAgger-distil it. *Measure:* Elo vs scripted baseline at matched CPU budget — the make-or-break number.
- **Days 4–5.** Sweep CEM horizon and candidate count to find the knee; add **PFSP-style weighting against your own past checkpoints**.
- **Days 6–7.** LLM-evolution over the *scoring/priority function only*, with dedup and evaluator-hack detection. *Measure:* wins vs the day-4 agent on held-out seeds.

Expected payoff ordering: **prior quality > bounded planning > opponent weighting > parameter tuning > market games**. If days 2–4 show no Elo gain, stop and invest entirely in the prior.
